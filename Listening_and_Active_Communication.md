# Listening and Active Communication

## Question 1

**What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?**

1. Give the speaker my full attention.
2. Avoid using my phone or other distractions while listening.
3. Do not interrupt the speaker.
4. Maintain appropriate eye contact and show that I am listening.
5. Pay attention to the speaker's tone, emotions, and body language.
6. Ask questions when I do not understand something.
7. Repeat or summarize the important points in my own words.
8. Avoid judging the speaker while they are explaining something.
9. Give the speaker enough time to finish their thoughts.
10. Confirm my understanding before responding or taking action.

---

## Question 2

**According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?**

Reflective listening means giving complete attention to the speaker, understanding their point of view, and reflecting their feelings and message back to them.

1. **Focus completely** - Remove distractions and concentrate on the conversation.
2. **Understand their point of view** - Try to understand what the other person means instead of thinking only about my own opinion.
3. **Do not judge quickly** - Listen with an open mind even if I disagree.
4. **Understand their emotions** - Pay attention to their feelings, tone, and body language.
5. **Reflect the message** - Repeat or explain the main idea in my own words.
6. **Verify my understanding** - Ask the person if I understood them correctly.

The main purpose is to make sure that what the speaker meant and what I understood are the same.

## Question 3

**What are the obstacles in your listening process?**

Some obstacles in my listening process are:

1. Getting distracted by my phone or other things around me.
2. Thinking about my response while the other person is still speaking.
3. Interrupting because I think I already know what they are going to say.
4. Losing concentration during long conversations.
5. Thinking about personal problems while someone is talking.
6. Making assumptions before listening to the complete message.
7. Getting affected by the speaker's tone or emotions.
8. Sometimes focusing more on the words than the actual meaning.
9. Not asking questions when I have doubts.
10. Feeling that I already understand something and not listening carefully.

---

## Question 4

**What can you do to improve your listening?**

I can improve my listening by:

1. Giving the speaker my complete attention.
2. Keeping my phone away during important conversations.
3. Avoiding interruptions.
4. Taking notes during technical discussions and meetings.
5. Asking questions whenever something is unclear.
6. Summarizing the important points after the conversation.
7. Paying attention to body language and emotions.
8. Avoiding assumptions and listening until the person finishes.
9. Practicing active listening in everyday conversations.
10. Confirming important information before taking action.

---

## Question 5

**When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

I sometimes use passive communication when I do not want to create conflict or disappoint someone.

For example:

- When I disagree with someone but do not express my opinion.
- When I accept extra work even though I am already busy.
- When I have a doubt but hesitate to ask.
- When someone makes a decision that I do not agree with, but I simply stay silent.
- When I want to say **"No"** but say **"Okay"** instead.
- When I avoid giving feedback because I am worried about how the other person will react.

---

## Question 6

**When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?**

I may use aggressive communication when I am frustrated, angry, or under pressure.

For example:

- When someone repeatedly interrupts me.
- When I feel that someone is not listening to my opinion.
- When I am under a lot of pressure and respond without thinking.
- When someone makes the same mistake repeatedly.
- When I strongly disagree with someone's opinion and focus more on proving myself right.
- When I raise my voice instead of calmly explaining my point.

Aggressive communication focuses too much on getting my own way and can make the other person feel disrespected.

---

## Question 7

**When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?**

I may use passive-aggressive communication when I am unhappy with something but do not communicate my feelings directly.

For example:

- Using sarcasm instead of directly explaining my problem.
- Giving someone the silent treatment.
- Complaining about someone to another person instead of talking to them directly.
- Giving taunts when I am angry.
- Saying **"It's okay"** even though I am actually upset.
- Deliberately delaying something because I am unhappy about it.
- Agreeing to something but showing my disagreement indirectly.

---

## Question 8

**How can you make your communication assertive?**

I can make my communication more assertive by:

1. **Speak clearly** - Say what I think or need without being rude.
2. **Respect others** - Listen to other people's opinions even when I disagree.
3. **Use "I" statements** - For example, say *"I feel this approach may cause a problem"* instead of *"You always do things wrong."*
4. **Say no when necessary** - Politely refuse something when I cannot take additional responsibility.
5. **Stay calm** - Avoid shouting or reacting immediately when I am angry.
6. **Be direct** - Discuss problems with the person involved instead of gossiping about them.
7. **Listen before responding** - Understand the other person's point before giving my opinion.
8. **Ask for clarification** - If I don't understand something, ask instead of making assumptions.
9. **Give constructive feedback** - Focus on the problem and possible solution rather than blaming the person.
10. **Think before speaking** - Take a moment to organize my thoughts before responding.

Assertive communication means being **clear and respectful while also standing up for my own thoughts, needs, and boundaries**.

---

## Simple Rule to Remember

| Communication Style | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Passive** | I don't express what I need. |
| **Aggressive** | I express what I need without respecting others. |
| **Passive-Aggressive** | I don't express it directly but show my anger indirectly. |
| **Assertive** | I clearly express what I need while respecting the other person. |
