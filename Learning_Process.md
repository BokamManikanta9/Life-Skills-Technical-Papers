# Learning Process

## 1. What is the Feynman Technique?

The Feynman Technique is a simple way to learn better by explaining a topic in your own easy words, as if you’re teaching it to someone who doesn’t know anything about it.



## 2. In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

In the video, the most interesting idea for me was how great scientists like Thomas Edison would handle problems when they felt stuck. Instead of getting frustrated and forcing themselves to think harder, they would relax and let their mind wander a little. Barbara Oakley explains that this helps the brain switch into a different thinking mode, where creative ideas can form naturally. I found this really inspiring because it shows that even brilliant people don’t always solve problems instantly — sometimes, taking a short break can actually help you think better and come up with new ideas.



## 3. What are Active and Diffused Modes of Thinking?

### Active (Focused) Mode:
- When you concentrate deeply on one task  
- Used for solving problems step by step  
- Helps in understanding details  

### Diffuse Mode:
- When your mind is relaxed  
- Happens during breaks, walking, or resting  
- Helps you see the big picture and get new ideas  



## 4. According to the video, what are the steps to take when approaching a new topic?

- Break the skill into small, important parts  
- Learn just enough about it to understand and correct mistakes  
- Remove distractions that stop practice  
- Practice consistently for at least 20 hours  



## 5. What actions can you take to improve your learning process?

- Study in small parts instead of everything at once  
- Practice daily, even for a short time  
- Explain what you learn in simple words  
- Take short breaks when stuck  
- Avoid distractions while studying  
- Focus on understanding, not just memorizing
